# Questions to ask them

**4 rounds, probably 4 different people. Match the question to the person.**
Pick **2–3 per round**. Never read a list out.

> **The best question references something they said 20 minutes earlier.** These are the backstop
> for when nothing comes to mind, not a script.

---

## Your three — keep all of them

**1 · Cloud, from the inside** *(technical rounds, or the case interviewer)*
> "Being cloud-native means you get new capabilities early. From the inside, what has actually been
> hard about that — what breaks, or what surprises people?"

*Trimmed from the long version. A short question gets a specific answer.*

**2 · AI in the model** *(follow-up to 1, or on its own)*
> "How does AI fit into how you build here — is it mostly assisted coding, or further into review
> and testing?"

*Your strongest, because you have a real stake. If they ask why: you built a harness for multi-repo
context and guardrails, because handing an AI a ticket with no context makes it invent conventions.*

**3 · The role** *(the hiring manager)*
> "What would a good first month look like, and a good first six? And what advice would you give
> someone joining, to do well here?"

*Keep the advice ending. People like answering it, and the answer is genuinely useful.*

---

## For an engineer

- "What does the on-call rotation look like, and how often does it actually page?"
- "When something breaks, what happens afterwards? Is there a postmortem, and does it change anything?"
- "How often do you deploy, and how much of that is automated?"
- "How does an infrastructure change get reviewed — same as application code, or a different path?"
- "Roughly how much of the week is project work versus keeping things running?"

*Every one of these doubles as evidence that you know the domain.*

## For the manager

- "How is the team structured, and where would I sit in it?"
- "What is the biggest thing the team is trying to fix in the next 6 months?"
- "How do you like to give feedback? I have found regular 1-1s useful — I asked for them in my last team." *(true, and it is story 2)*
- "What separates someone doing this job well from someone doing it very well?"

## For anyone

- "What made you stay?"
- "What surprised you most in your first few months?"
- "What is the hardest part of this job that would not be obvious from the outside?"

---

## Do not ask

| Avoid | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Anything on the careers page | Signals you did not look |
| Salary, holiday, perks | The recruiter, not the interviewer |
| "Do you have any concerns about me?" | Invites a negative close. High risk, low reward. |
| A long multi-part question | It gets answered vaguely, or only the last part gets answered |

---

## If you genuinely have nothing left

Do not invent one. **Say something true instead:**

> "You covered most of what I had — especially the part about `[X]`. The thing I am still curious
> about is `[Y]`."

That reads far better than a filler question.
