Case Round Cheat Sheet

Consulting format, engineering content. Framing → drivers → recommendation. Draw while you talk. Numbers out loud. Recommendations may change.

The arc

  1. Clarify. 3–4 questions before you draw. Who uses it? Read or write heavy? What must never break? Is the spike predictable?
  2. Numbers. Out loud. "Let me put rough numbers on this first." Rough is fine; silent is not.
  3. Boxes and arrows. Simplest thing that works. No product names yet.
  4. Name the bottleneck. "The constraint here is the database, so that is where I would spend the design."
  5. Tradeoffs as you go. Gain and lose, every time.
  6. Recommend — and say what would change your mind.

The payment spike — their example

  1. Predictable or not? Payday and Black Friday are schedulable. Viral is not. Different answers.
  2. Numbers. Baseline vs peak, and how long the peak lasts. 10× for 5 min ≠ 2× for 6 h.
  3. Decouple accepting from processing. Accept, write to a queue, return "accepted". Workers drain it at their own rate. The spike hits the queue, not the database. A queue is a shock absorber — strongest single thing to say.
  4. Idempotency keys, so a retry never double-charges. Say this unprompted.
  5. Autoscaling warms up in minutes, not seconds. Predictable → scale on a schedule, ahead of it. Unpredictable → keep headroom, scale on queue depth, not CPU.
  6. Protect the database. It cannot scale in seconds. Connection pooling, read replicas, queue absorbing writes.
  7. Shed load deliberately. Rate limit, return a clear "try again". A degraded service beats a dead one.
  8. Cost: reserved for the baseline, on-demand for the spike. Do not pay peak prices all month.

"The API is slow" — the ladder

Ask in this order. Never guess first.

  1. Slow for whom? All users, one region, one endpoint?
  2. p50 or p99? p50 slow = everything. p99 slow = contention, GC, or a cold cache.
  3. When did it start? A deploy, a traffic change, or gradual?
  4. Where is the time? A trace answers this — app, database, or a downstream call.
  5. Then the usual suspects: a missing index, N+1 queries, no connection pool, no cache, a chatty dependency with no timeout.

"I would look at a trace before changing anything — I want to know which hop owns the latency."

Monitoring & alerts

Metricsnumbers over time. cheap, aggregate
Logsevents with detail. expensive
Tracesone request across services

SLI the measurement · SLO your target · SLA the contract, money attached.

Error budget = 100% − SLO. 99.9% = 43 min/month. Spend it on releases. Budget gone → stop shipping features.

"Alert on symptoms, not causes. Checkout error rate above 2% — not CPU at 90%."

Incident: detect → mitigate → fix → learn. Mitigate before you fix — roll back first, understand afterwards.

"Multi-AZ is the default and it is cheap insurance. Multi-region I would justify by a real requirement — a compliance rule, a latency target, or a stated RTO — not by instinct."

RPO = data you can lose. RTO = time you can be down. Ask for both numbers.

Scaling — in order

  1. Stateless app tier. Always first. Nothing else works until session state is out of the app.
  2. Load balancer + horizontal scaling
  3. Cache — reads repeat. Costs you staleness.
  4. Read replicas — costs you replication lag, so reads can be stale
  5. Queue — absorbs write spikes, costs eventual consistency
  6. CDN for static and global
  7. Shard only when one database is genuinely full

Cost levers

  1. Right-size first. Most bills are oversized instances, not too many.
  2. Reserved / savings plan for the steady baseline — up to ~40% off.
  3. Spot for anything interruptible — batch, CI, workers.
  4. Autoscale down, not just up. That is elasticity.
  5. Storage tiers — move cold objects off standard.
  6. Egress. $0.09/GB. Cross-AZ and cross-region traffic is the line nobody predicts.
  7. Managed vs self-hosted — more per hour, less per engineer.

Migration to the cloud

The three options, in cost order:

Lift and shiftfast, low risk, no cloud benefit — and often more expensive than the datacenter
Replatformmanaged database, containers. the usual right answer.
Refactorserverless, microservices. biggest gain, biggest cost and risk.

"I would lift and shift what is stable, replatform what is painful, and refactor only where the numbers justify it. And I would move something low-risk first, to learn."

Gain: elasticity, speed, no capacity planning, managed services.
Lose: per-hour cost can exceed owned hardware at steady load, egress fees, lock-in, and a new skill requirement.

The distinctions they listed

Scalability vs elasticityScalability = can it grow. Elasticity = grows AND shrinks, automatically, fast. They asked for both words.
Performance vs efficiencyPerformance = how fast. Efficiency = how much you spent to get it.
Resilience vs availabilityAvailability = up now. Resilience = recovers from failure.
Consistency vs availabilityIn a partition you pick one. Payments pick consistency.
Latency vs throughputBatching raises both.
AgilityHow fast you can change your mind. Managed services and IaC buy it.

Say these

  • "Let me put rough numbers on this first."
  • "Before I design — is the spike predictable, or genuinely unexpected?"
  • "The bottleneck here is X, so that is where I would spend the design."
  • "I would accept and queue, so the spike hits the queue rather than the database."
  • "Idempotency keys, so a retry during a spike never double-charges."
  • "That is the simplest thing that works — I would add more only when the numbers justify it."
  • "What are your RPO and RTO? That decides multi-AZ versus multi-region."
  • "I would look at a trace before changing anything."
  • "I would change this recommendation if X turned out to be true."
  • "Can I take a moment to think about that?"
