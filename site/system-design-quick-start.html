Quick start · system design

Design a system from a blank page

The order to think in, the numbers to reach for, the checks to run before you stop, and the mistakes one attempt actually made. The short form of the framework, for the minute before you draw.

8 min read

01 · Why an order

A design fails on order before it fails on knowledge.

You know the components. A blank page and a prompt still stall, because nothing tells you what to say first. This guide gives the order, the numbers, and the checks. It does not replace FRAMEWORK.md. It is the one page you open before you draw.

The loop has 3 stages. Each stage ends in a file that you wrote. The stages are independent of the weekday. If a stage needs another slot, continue it in the next slot. Do not restart the exercise, and do not add hours.

Stage What you do The artifact
AttemptClarify the prompt, estimate, draw, and narrate. Ask for an unknown concept when you hit it.attempt.md and attempt.png
EvaluationExplain your intent box by box. Compare choices. Run the 3×3 sweep.gaps.md, one line per question you could not answer
Gap closurePick one gap. Learn it with numbers and a diagram that you draw.A note in concepts/

Source · system-design/FRAMEWORK.md, the three stages, 2026-09-10 revision

An unfinished attempt is a valid artifact. A bad one-page design beats a well-understood article, because the design shows you what you could not answer, and the article does not.

02 · Before you draw

Four things are on the desk before the first box.

Set up takes 2 minutes and removes the first stall. Everything below is already in the repo.

  1. 01The prompt, read twice. Copy the constraints given up front into your notes before you think about a solution.
  2. 02Excalidraw, open on a blank page. Draw by hand. The skill is to narrate while you draw, and a tool that arranges boxes for you trains the wrong skill.
  3. 03The attempt template, copied to attempt.md in the problem folder. Leave unknown parts open. The template guides the artifact, not the conversation.
  4. 04The anchors table in section 04, or your memory of it. You will need one anchor in the first 5 minutes.
Note

A timer is optional. A guided attempt has no clock, and the mentor asks about your time only when you request a timed session. If you do want pressure, say so, and agree the duration before you start.

03 · The order

Say the numbers before you draw the boxes.

Follow this order out loud. It is a method, not a checklist: it tells you what to think about next. The checklist in section 06 tells you what you forgot, and it comes later for that reason.

Step Say out loud Minutes of 40
1 · Clarify3 or 4 questions before you draw. Who uses it? Read heavy or write heavy? What must never break? Write the answer you assume when nobody is there to ask.5
2 · Numbers"Let me put rough numbers on this first." Write the arithmetic, not only the result. Rough is fine. Silent is not.5
3 · Boxes and arrowsThe simplest thing that works. No product names yet. Say what each box is responsible for as you draw it.10
4 · The bottleneck"The constraint here is X, so that is where I spend the design." If the numbers say there is no bottleneck, say that, with the arithmetic.15
5 · TradeoffsFor every choice: what you gain, what you lose, and what you rejected.throughout
6 · RecommendState the design you would build. Then say what evidence would change your mind.5

Illustrative · the minute budgets fit a 40-minute slot and are a guide, not a rule. Method from FRAMEWORK.md, 2026-09-04 version.

Caution

The most common failure is a design for a scale you never estimated. Do step 2 before step 3, every time. A queue, a shard, or a second region that no number asked for is a guess with a product name on it.

04 · The numbers

Every rate comes from one division, and one anchor checks it.

Requests per day divided by 86,400 seconds gives requests per second. One anchor checks the result: 1 million requests a day is about 12 a second. If your answer is 100 times off, the anchor catches it before the design inherits the error.

The anchors

Anchor Value What it decides
1M requests a day≈ 12 / sThe size class of the system. Most designs are smaller than they feel.
100M requests a day≈ 1,200 / sStill one modest server for simple reads.
Peak over average2 to 5×The capacity you provision. State the multiplier you assume.
Memory read~100 nsWhy a cache in front of a database is worth its complexity.
SSD read~100 µs1,000 times slower than memory. The database floor.
Network, same region~0.5 msA hop you can afford several times per request.
Network, cross-region50 to 150 msKills synchronous multi-region. One round trip is the whole latency budget.
One SQL database~5k to 10k simple reads / sWhen you need a cache or a replica. Writes are far fewer.
Cache hit rate80 to 95 %How much read load reaches the database. A drop from 97 % to 80 % is 7× more database reads.
Data out of AWS~$0.09 / GBThe cost line nobody predicts. Egress, not compute, is where a read-heavy design pays.

Illustrative · order of magnitude only. Each anchor is an assumption to check against real hardware, a real price list, or measured traffic before it supports a decision.

The worked example: the URL shortener

The prompt gives 100 million new links a year and 100 reads for each write. The table shows the arithmetic in full, because the arithmetic is the artifact. The last two rows use 2 different storage assumptions and reach the same conclusion. That is what a robust estimate looks like.

Quantity Working Result What it decides
Writes per second100,000,000 / 365 / 24 / 3,6003.2No queue on the write path. 3 writes a second needs no shock absorber.
Reads per second3.2 × 100~320One database survives with the cache down. Replication is not required by load.
Peak reads per second320 × 3~1,000One modest server. Multi-region is not justified by throughput.
Storage, 5 years, at 500 B a row100M × 5 × 500 B250 GBFits one machine. No sharding. Revisit near 2 TB.
Storage, 5 years, at 1 kB a row100M × 5 × 1 kB500 GBThe same conclusion. The estimate is robust to a 2× error in the row size.

Source · the prompt in problems/01-url-shortener/PROBLEM.md; arithmetic from reference.md, 2026-09-10. The 500 B row is the reference assumption. The 1 kB row is the learner's own assumption from attempt.md.

The real attempt wrote 100,000,000 / 12 as 83k. The anchor would have caught it in one step: 8.33M a month is 274k a day, and 274k is about a quarter of 1M, so about 3 a second. Every number downstream inherited the slip, and the design never learned how small the system was.

05 · The trace

One request through the drawing proves the drawing.

A diagram is a claim. A trace tests it. Pick one request, start at the client, and name what each box does to the request until the client has its answer. A box you cannot narrate is a box you do not need yet.

  1. 01A box is a responsibility or a separate deployment. Say which one you mean. A responsibility can live inside another box's process.
  2. 02An arrow shows who starts the request. The reply travels back on the same arrow without a second arrowhead. A bidirectional arrow hides the initiator, and security groups, NAT, and webhooks all turn on who initiates.
  3. 03Label the arrow with the verb and the payload. "GET code" tells the reader more than a line does.
Note

The last arrow carries a decision. A 301 lets the browser cache the redirect, so you carry less load and lose every click after the first. A 302 sends every click through you, so analytics work and every click costs a request. Say which one you pick, and why.

06 · The sweep

Nine cells find what the trace missed.

Traffic, compute, and data are layers. Security, cost, and observability are lenses over all three. Each cell is a question about this design. A blank cell is visible: "I have said nothing about cost at the data layer" is a thought you can have.

Layer Security Cost Observability
TrafficTLS, abuse controls, access rulesEgress and request volumeRequest rate, latency, error rate
ComputeLeast privilege and service accessCapacity and utilizationCPU, saturation, request traces
DataAccess control and encryptionStorage, indexes, retentionSlow queries, queue depth, replication lag

Source · the design checklist in system-design/FRAMEWORK.md, 2026-09-10 revision

Mark a cell irrelevant only with a reason. Never add a component to fill a cell. The grid also does not replace 5 checks that sit outside it.

  1. 01Confirm the requirements and the estimates.
  2. 02Trace one real API request through the data operations.
  3. 03Identify transaction boundaries and what happens on a duplicate request.
  4. 04Explain timeouts, retries, partial failures, and recovery.
  5. 05State a recommendation with gain-versus-lose tradeoffs.
Tip

Run the sweep out loud when you think you are finished. Most designs stop at "it works and it scales" and never mention cost or security. A metric is a number over time, so "the database is up" fills no cell. "Cache hit rate" does.

07 · Failure modes

These are the mistakes one attempt actually produced.

Each row is a sentence from the first URL shortener attempt or its evaluation. The first move is what to do the moment you hear yourself say it.

What you said The cause First move
"100M divided by 12 is 83k."Arithmetic without an anchor. The 100× slip passed into every later number.Check the result against 1M a day ≈ 12 a second before you move on.
"The database is up." as a metric.A state, not a number. It tells you after the failure, never before.Name a number over time: p99 redirect latency, error rate, cache hit rate.
"Add replication to distribute the load."A component chosen because load felt scary, not because a number asked for it.Say the read rate first. 320 reads a second needs no replica.
"Maybe it was a region outage."The scenario widened instead of answered. It reads as a dodge even when it is not.Answer the question as asked, in full. Then extend if you want.
Every arrow drawn with two heads.Arrows drawn as information flow instead of as who initiates.Redraw with one head, from the caller. Label the verb.
"It hides the real URL, so people cannot see it." as a feature.The attack surface was not flipped. A hidden destination is a phishing tool.Ask once per design: who benefits from this if they are malicious? Then add the control.
The bottleneck section left blank.Scale assumed, not estimated, so there was nothing to say.At this scale the answer is "there is none, and here is the arithmetic." That is a strong answer.

08 · After the session

A session that leaves no artifact did not happen.

Reading feels productive. Retrieval is what works. The DSA tracker proved this once already. These 5 steps are how the design loop keeps the same discipline.

  1. 01Save attempt.md and attempt.png. An unfinished attempt is valid.
  2. 02Write gaps.md: one line for each question you could not answer. It is the curriculum, and it only holds what you actually hit.
  3. 03Record the stage and the next action in PROGRESS.md, so the next session resumes instead of restarts.
  4. 04On Friday, pick one gap. Not three. Learn it with a worked example with numbers and a diagram that you draw.
  5. 05Write that concept into system-design/concepts/<slug>.md, and record it in TOPICS.md with the artifact as evidence.

Next: open the URL shortener sketch and trace the read path out loud, box by box. That trace is the open stage in PROGRESS.md, and it is where the next gap will show itself.
